01

People first.
Robots second.

I am a Doctoral Researcher in Human–Technology Interaction at Tampere University. My background sits between psychology, HCI and robotics, which means I am usually less interested in whether a robot can do something than in what happens when a person has to work alongside it.

My doctoral research focuses on trust in human–robot teaming, particularly in mixed-fleet logistics environments where people, automated guided vehicles, mobile robots and other technologies share the same work.

I use quantitative, qualitative and experimental methods — from interviews and questionnaires to Unity simulations and physical robot studies.

02

Research

My PhD asks how trust develops and changes through interaction — and how robot behaviour, communication and training can support appropriate trust and safe collaboration.

01 / Survey

Trust in warehouse automation

Examining attitudes toward automated guided vehicles and how trust differs across workers and generations.

Human–automation trust · AGVs · Logistics
02 / Simulation

Trust after first contact

A Unity-based mixed-fleet warehouse study investigating how direct experience changes trust and how multimodal communication can support interaction.

Simulation · Trust dynamics · Multimodal HRI
03 / Interviews

Working with unclear automation

Exploring how workers coordinate, interpret uncertainty and build conditional trust when automation is not fully legible.

Qualitative HRI · Uncertainty · Coordination
04 / Multi-robot study

One task, multiple trust targets

Research at Bristol Robotics Laboratory combining an upper-body exoskeleton with a mobile robot to examine workload, usefulness and target-specific trust.

Exoskeletons · Mobile robots · Workload

“Trust is not one thing aimed at one machine. In real collaboration, people continuously evaluate different technologies, behaviours, interfaces and their own ability to respond.”

03

Portfolio

A selection of research, interactive prototypes and creative projects, bringing together psychology, design and hands-on experimentation.

Research / Unity simulation

Sharing a warehouse with robots

A Unity-based warehouse simulation exploring how people navigate shared spaces with automated guided vehicles. Scenarios include narrow corridors, intersections and deadlocks.

The research examines how direct experience changes trust and how people adapt their behaviour around automation.

Unity · Experimental research · Human–robot interaction
Research / Multimodal interaction

Making robot activity easier to understand

Exploring haptic feedback, scent cues and a visual map as ways to support awareness in a simulated mixed-fleet warehouse.

The study considers which signals people notice and find useful when coordinating their movement with automated vehicles.

Interaction design · Multimodal feedback · User evaluation
Research / Physical robotics

One task, multiple technologies

A collaborative study at Bristol Robotics Laboratory involving an upper-body exoskeleton, a Unitree GO2 mobile robot and a shared box-handling task.

The research explores trust, workload and perceived usefulness when wearable assistance and mobile robotics become part of the same activity.

Exoskeletons · Mobile robots · Mixed-methods research
MSc research / Co-design

Designing interaction with social robots

My MSc research explored social robot interaction through co-design involving NAO and Elias.

The work brought psychological understanding together with participatory design and evaluation of interactive technology.

Co-design · Social robotics · Human–technology interaction
