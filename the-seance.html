The Séance

The Séance is a gothic logic puzzle game for a vertical mobile screen. Players arrange a séance by interpreting cryptic rules and manipulating the people and objects around a ritual table.

Solving a puzzle means deciding which candles should burn, where characters should look and how their bodies should be positioned. Once everything is ready, the player calls the spirit to test the arrangement.

The concept

Each puzzle presents a scene and a set of conditions for conducting the ritual. The player must make every condition true at the same time.

The characters’ positions are part of the puzzle. Turning someone’s head, moving an arm away from the table or extinguishing a candle can satisfy one condition while breaking another. Players need to consider how these actions affect the whole scene before making the call.

The intended interaction is direct: manipulate the scene itself to construct the answer.

How a puzzle works

An illustrative puzzle could place four characters around the table: the Widow, the Child, the Soldier and the Priest. Each has a candle, and certain characters have adjustable poses.

The ritual instructions

Exactly two candles must burn.

The Child’s candle must burn, and the Widow must look away from it.

The Priest’s candle must remain unlit, and his gaze must stay on the table.

The Widow’s candle may burn only while the Soldier touches the table.

While the Priest watches the table, the Soldier must keep his hand away from it.

Reveal the solution

To prepare the scene, the player must:

  • Light the Child’s and Soldier’s candles.
  • Leave the Widow’s and Priest’s candles unlit.
  • Turn the Widow’s head away from the Child’s candle.
  • Position the Priest looking towards the table.
  • Move the Soldier’s hand off the table.

The conditions connect logically: the Priest must watch the table, so the Soldier cannot touch it. That prevents the Widow’s candle from burning. With the Priest’s candle also forbidden, the Soldier’s candle is the only possible second flame.

The player then calls the spirit to check whether the scene satisfies every rule. This example illustrates the intended puzzle structure; the individual puzzles are still being developed.

Interaction and atmosphere

I’m developing the opening ritual around candle states, gaze direction and body positioning. These give players different ways to change the scene while keeping the puzzle grounded in the séance.

The visual design needs to make those states readable. A player should be able to distinguish a hand resting on the table from one held away, or see clearly which direction a character is looking. On a mobile screen, those differences need to remain visible without losing the atmosphere of the illustration.

Gothic artwork, animated titles and Victorian-inspired character designs establish the setting. The ritual instructions carry that tone while still needing to communicate precise, solvable conditions.

Character design

The characters contribute both to the story’s atmosphere and to its mechanics. Their silhouettes, expressions and poses need to give them distinct identities while making their interactive states easy to recognise.

The Doctor, Child, Soldier and Priest are the characters designed so far. The Widow in the illustrative puzzle is planned for later. The cast and puzzles are still evolving as part of this personal hobby project.

Development in Unity

This is an ongoing personal project combining game design, 2D interaction and digital art. I’m developing it in Unity for a vertical mobile layout, with the menu and opening candle ritual providing the starting point.

The next design challenge is refining how players manipulate the characters and how the game responds to a ritual attempt. That feedback needs to help players understand the result while leaving room for them to work out the solution.
